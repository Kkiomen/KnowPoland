Warsaw

Warszawa · Mazovia · the rebuilt capital

Part 01

The city that was rebuilt
from paintings

Warsaw was deliberately destroyed after the 1944 uprising - street by street, on orders. What you walk through today was put back between 1945 and 1984 using surviving fragments, pre-war photographs and 18th-century cityscapes by Bernardo Bellotto. The Old Town is medieval in shape and post-war in fact.

Known forReconstruction, glass towers, the best museums in the country
Go forThe Warsaw Rising Museum and POLIN, the museum of Polish Jews
Nobody tells youThe ghetto boundary is marked in the pavement. You will cross it without noticing.

Part 02

The one the war
left standing

Kraków kept its medieval centre, its royal castle on Wawel hill and a market square the size of a small district. It is also an hour from Auschwitz-Birkenau, and the two facts belong to the same city - which is why we treat the second as history, not as a day trip.

Known forWawel, the Cloth Hall, Kazimierz - the old Jewish quarter
Go forEurope's oldest continuously operating salt mine, at Wieliczka
Nobody tells youA trumpet call sounds from the church tower every hour, and breaks off mid-note. On purpose.

Part 03

Where the war started,
and where it ended

The first shots of the Second World War were fired here in September 1939. Forty-one years later, a strike in the same city's shipyard began the end of communism in Europe. The merchant houses along the main street look 17th-century and were rebuilt in the 1950s.

Known forThe Baltic, amber, cranes, and Solidarity
Go forThe European Solidarity Centre, built against the shipyard gate
Nobody tells youFor centuries it was a semi-independent city, German-speaking as often as Polish. That argument is the whole region's history.

Part 04

The city that changed
country without moving

Until 1945 this was Breslau, a German city. After the war the borders shifted west, the German population left or was expelled, and people arrived from Polish towns that had just become Soviet. An entire city was replaced, population and all, within a few years.

Known forIslands, a hundred bridges, and a market square rebuilt in colour
Go forThe Racławice Panorama - a 15-metre-high painting in the round
Nobody tells youThere are several hundred small bronze dwarves hidden around the streets. They started as anti-communist street protest.

Part 05

Alps, compressed
into fifty kilometres

The Tatras are small, steep and shared with Slovakia. Rysy, the highest point in Poland, reaches 2,499 metres. Below them sits Zakopane, a town with its own architectural style, its own dialect and a highlander culture that predates the modern state.

Known forGranite peaks, mountain huts, sheep's cheese smoked over a fire
Go forA full-day ridge walk in summer, or the wooden churches in the valleys
Nobody tells youWeather turns fast and mountain rescue is called out often. Check the forecast the way locals do.

Part 06

The forest Europe
forgot to cut down

On the eastern border stands one of the last large remnants of the primeval lowland forest that once covered the continent. It is the home of the European bison, brought back from near-extinction after the First World War, and it is split in half by the Polish-Belarusian border.

Known forBison, thousand-year-old oaks, strict-protection zones
Go forA guided dawn walk - the strict reserve cannot be entered alone
Nobody tells youThe surrounding region is Orthodox as often as Catholic, with wooden churches to match.

And the century behind all six

Four dates that explain
everything you just read

Every one of these places was shaped by the same short list of years. Start anywhere - each entry takes about five minutes and links to the next.

1795

Erased

Three empires divide the country. No Poland on the map for 123 years.

 1939

Invasion

Germany on 1 September, the Soviet Union on the 17th. Occupation follows.

 1945

Moved west

New borders, emptied cities, and millions of people resettled.

 1989

The turn

Partly free elections. Everything modern about Poland dates from here.
