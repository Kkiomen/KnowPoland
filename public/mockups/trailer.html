CHAPTER 01

The baptism

966 · Gniezno

A duke named Mieszko accepts Christianity, and a cluster of Slavic tribes becomes a country inside Latin Europe - Latin script, Latin law, a western alphabet for a Slavic language. Nearly every argument about where Poland belongs starts from this single decision.

Where you can stand in it

Gniezno Cathedral - the coronation church of the first Polish kings, with bronze doors from the 1170s.

CHAPTER 02

The republic
of nobles

1569-1795 · The Commonwealth

Poland and Lithuania merge into one of the largest states in Europe, with an elected king and a parliament where a single deputy could veto everything. It writes Europe's first modern codified constitution on 3 May 1791 - and is wiped off the map four years later.

Where you can stand in it

The Royal Castle in Warsaw, where the constitution was adopted - itself a post-war reconstruction.

CHAPTER 03

Erased

1795-1918 · 123 years without a state

Russia, Prussia and Austria divide the country between them. There is no Poland on any map for four generations - but there are Polish books, songs, uprisings and secret schools, which is why the language and the idea are still here when the empires collapse in 1918.

Where you can stand in it

Any 19th-century city centre: Kraków, Poznań and Warsaw were built by three different empires, and still look it.

CHAPTER 04

1939

1 September 1939 · The war starts here

Germany invades from the west; the Soviet Union from the east on 17 September. The occupation that follows kills millions of Polish citizens, including almost the entire pre-war Jewish population. Warsaw ends the war as rubble. This chapter is not a sightseeing stop - it is the reason the country looks the way it does.

How to visit it properly

Westerplatte, the Warsaw Rising Museum, Auschwitz-Birkenau - book ahead, go quietly, leave the camera in your bag.

CHAPTER 05

Solidarity

1980-1989 · Gdańsk, and then everywhere

Shipyard workers strike and win an independent trade union - ten million members inside a year. Martial law follows in December 1981, but the argument is already lost. In June 1989 partly free elections hand the opposition every seat it is allowed to win, and the rest of the bloc follows within months.

Where you can stand in it

The European Solidarity Centre, built against the shipyard gate where the strike demands were nailed up.

CHAPTER 06

The country
you'll land in

1989 - today

Card payments on every bus, intercity trains that arrive on time, mountains in the south and two thousand lakes in the north-east. The currency is the złoty and not the euro, the milk bars still serve lunch for the price of a coffee in Berlin, and the whole country is noticeably younger and faster than its own reputation abroad.

Practical, checked monthly

Money, trains, phone data, tipping, pharmacy hours - the short version, with dates on it.
