STOP 01Start · Baltic coast

Gdańsk

The port that started and ended the century

The first shots of the Second World War were fired here in September 1939. Forty-one years later, a strike in the shipyard began the end of communism in Europe. The tall merchant houses on the main street look seventeenth-century and were rebuilt in the 1950s from ruins.

Why it's stop one

Because the two events that define modern Poland happened within three kilometres of each other, here.

STOP 02170 km · on the Vistula

Toruń

Gothic brick, and the man who moved the Earth

A medieval trading town that the war left almost untouched - a whole city centre of red brick, unreconstructed. Nicolaus Copernicus was born here in 1473; he would publish the argument that the Earth orbits the Sun in the year he died.

Why it's on the route

It shows you what the rebuilt cities were rebuilt to look like. Also: gingerbread, baked here since the Middle Ages.

STOP 03380 km · the capital

Warsaw

Rebuilt from photographs and paintings

Deliberately destroyed after the 1944 uprising, then put back between 1945 and 1984 using surviving fragments and eighteenth-century cityscapes. The Old Town is medieval in shape and post-war in fact. Around it stands the newest skyline in this part of Europe.

Don't skip

POLIN, the museum of the history of Polish Jews, and the ghetto boundary marked in the pavement outside it.

STOP 04730 km · Lower Silesia

Wrocław

The city that changed country without moving

Until 1945 this was Breslau, and German. Then the borders shifted west, the population was expelled, and people arrived from Polish towns that had just become Soviet. An entire city was replaced, inhabitants and all, inside a few years - and it is now one of the most cheerful places in the country.

Look down

Several hundred small bronze dwarves are hidden around the streets. They began as anti-communist street protest.

STOP 051 000 km · Lesser Poland

Kraków

The one the war left standing

Royal castle on a hill, a market square the size of a district, and a former Jewish quarter that is now the busiest part of town. It is also an hour from Auschwitz-Birkenau - which belongs to this stop as history, not as an excursion, and should be booked and entered accordingly.

Underground

Wieliczka: a salt mine worked continuously from the thirteenth century until 1996, with chapels carved in salt.

STOP 061 100 km · end of the road

Zakopane

Where the country runs out of flat

A mountain town with its own wooden architecture, its own dialect and a highlander culture older than the modern state. Above it the Tatras rise to 2 499 metres at Rysy - small, steep and genuinely alpine, with weather that turns in twenty minutes and a mountain rescue service that is called out constantly.

Before you go up

Trails are graded and often chained. Check the forecast and the avalanche level, and start early.
