The same country, four times over

It is not one

Ask when to come to Poland and you'll get four different answers, because the country genuinely rearranges itself: −15 °C and dark at four in the afternoon, or 30 °C and light until ten. Scroll through the year before you pick your month.

SEASON 01December - February

Winter

zima

Short days, long nights and a country that moves indoors. The mountains fill with skiers, the cities smell of smoke and mulled wine, and Christmas Eve - not Christmas Day - is the evening everything stops for: twelve meatless dishes, an empty place set at the table, and no shops open on the 25th or 26th.

SEASON 02March - May

Spring

wiosna

Everything happens at once: the parks fill within a week, the storks come back to the north-east, and the calendar stacks up. Easter Monday is a national water fight - strangers included, and the first days of May bring two public holidays back to back, which empties the cities and fills the mountains.

SEASON 03June - August

Summer

lato

Light until ten at night, the whole country outdoors, and the coast and lakes full of Poles rather than foreigners. It is the loudest, warmest and most expensive quarter - and the only one in which the Baltic is arguably swimmable. Expect thunderstorms in the afternoon and long, slow evenings afterwards.

SEASON 04September - November

Autumn

jesień

The best light of the year, mushroom season in the forests - a genuine national pastime, not a metaphor - and on 1 November a country lit by candles: All Saints' Day empties the motorways and fills every cemetery, including the ones nobody has family in any more. If you want to understand how Poland relates to its dead, come in November.
